Elder Conversation Guide — Grace Community Church, Huntsville

What This Is & How It Works

Every full-time staff member at GCC is also an elder, so this review holds two things together: his character and personal wellbeing, and how he's actually doing in each ministry he oversees. It's meant to be honest about growth areas — but just as intent on naming real strengths and fruit, not only cataloging what needs work. This particular document is the conversation guide, not the formal scored review — it's meant to be worked through together, out loud, live, with encouragement first on purpose.

Documents in this process:

  • staff-self-review.html — the staff elder's own honest self-assessment, completed alone.
  • elder-assessment.html — the coming-alongside elder's formal, signed evaluation; the official record for the personnel file.
  • elder-conversation-guide.html — a shared, unscored discussion guide the two of them work through together, live.

The steps:

  1. The staff member completes staff-self-review.html on his own, honestly.
  2. He clicks "Download progress (.json)" there — that just saves a small file to his computer, it doesn't send it anywhere by itself.
  3. He sends that file to his coming-alongside elder himself — email, AirDrop, Slack, whatever's easiest.
  4. The elder loads that file into elder-assessment.html as reference, before writing anything.
  5. The two of them meet and work through this document together — live, unscored, encouragement first.
  6. The elder completes his formal assessment in elder-assessment.html, informed by both the self-review and the conversation.
  7. Both sign off at the bottom of elder-assessment.html, and it's printed to PDF for the personnel file.
  8. At the 6-month mark, they revisit the Check-In section in elder-assessment.html for a lighter pulse — not a full redo.

Bringing It Together

A robust review holds both sides at once — say the good plainly, and be specific about where growth is needed. Both halves of this belong in the file.
